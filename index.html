The job: Each morning it finds the cheapest round-trip economy fare from Singapore to Beijing Capital across six date pairs (leave 21–23 Dec, return 9–10 Jan) and emails it to the traveller, a student booking a winter-break trip, marking ALERT when the fare is below CNY 1,600 or cheaper than the last check.
  2. The trigger: a new row in my Google Sheet "Daily fares", which a Google Apps Script adds at about 9 am Singapore time each day after searching Google Flights through SerpApi.
  3. The tools: Google Sheets (trigger: New Spreadsheet Row) and Email by Zapier (action: Send Outbound Email); upstream, a Google Apps Script calling SerpApi's read-only Google Flights search.
  4. It must never: book, hold or pay for a ticket; no booking or payment app is connected, and its only action is Send Outbound Email to my own address.
  5. Where a person checks: I, the traveller, read each email after the 9 am run, open its Google Flights link to spot-check the cheapest fare, dates and flight before acting on an ALERT, and compare each run with its record in Zap history.

Q1 · Where my agent sits, and what it means for the person in the loop

(a) My quadrant. AI alone, with checks (bottom left).

(b) The two questions. It is work with a right answer: the job is to find the cheapest round-trip economy fare across six fixed date pairs and flag it when it is below CNY 1,600 or cheaper than the last check, and every answer can be checked against Google Flights. Two checks within the same hour on 18 Oct both returned CNY 2,640 for 21 Dec to 9 Jan, while two days earlier the cheapest fare had been CNY 2,907: the answer changes from day to day, but on any given day there is one. Evidence to add: the Google Flights link for run 1 showed the same fare. It is work that either of them can do alone: in run 1 the script wrote its row and the Zap emailed it at 03:01 with no step waiting for me, and I could do the same six searches by hand in about ten minutes.

(c) The person in the loop. I am the person at the gate, before any money is spent. I read the email after each 9 am run, and before acting on an ALERT I open its Google Flights link and check the fare, dates and flight; I also compare each run with its record in Zap history. The agent may search and email me on its own, and it can never book or pay, because no booking or payment app is connected. It would move up, to A person leads, AI inside, if it could book, because I would then have to sign off each booking. It would move right, to A person decides, AI alongside, if the job became choosing the best trip, trading a lower fare against an extra day at home, Daxing against Capital or a cheaper connection against a nonstop, because that has no single right answer.

(d) My group's view. I agree with our group placing task 4, Internship Alerts on Mail, in AI alone: like my agent, it matches openings against fixed criteria and emails the result for a person to spot-check. I would answer the together-or-alone question differently for task 2, the VIP notification hub: flagging which messages come from VIP senders has a right answer and an agent can do it alone, so I would place it in AI alone, with checks, and treat the replies that need a person's voice as a separate job. Looking back at my own Week 7 task, news summarisation (task 3), I would now answer the right-answer question differently too: the facts have a right answer, but judging their impact on us does not, which puts that part in A person decides, AI alongside rather than A person leads, AI inside.

Reflection

My agent fitted the job because checking six fixed date pairs every morning is tedious for me but has one right answer that the agent can find alone. It failed silently when every search failed: until the fix, a bad day produced no row and no email, which I could have read as "no change". I would add the return flight and a week's price trend to the email, and I would test the failure case before the first live run.
